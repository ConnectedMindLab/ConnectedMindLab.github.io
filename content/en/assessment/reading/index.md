## What is the Eye Movement Based Reading Assessment?

The Eye Movement Based Reading Assessment uses eye-tracking technology to measure and analyze eye movements in real time while reading. Beyond simply scoring reading performance, it measures fixation count, fixation duration, regression count, binocular coordination, reading speed, and blink patterns to provide objective data on an individual's reading habits and cognitive strengths and weaknesses.

Based on this data, reading level is compared against age-group norms, helping to accurately diagnose the underlying causes of reading difficulties. It also enables the design of an optimized, personalized training program and the development of effective reading strategies.

## Understanding Cognitive Processes Through Eye Movement Analysis

Reading is not merely the act of scanning text with the eyes — it is an activity in which complex cognitive processes occur simultaneously. As we read, our eyes move in consistent patterns, and these eye movements directly reflect the brain's activity in comprehending and processing text.

### The Relationship Between Eye Movements and Cognitive Processing

When reading, eye movements fall into two main categories:

**Fixation**: The moment the eye pauses and focuses on a word. During this brief interval, the brain recognizes the text and extracts meaning. When reading speed is slow or comprehension is poor, fixation duration increases and fixation count rises.

**Saccade**: The rapid movement of the eye from one word to the next. Unlike fixations, saccades do not involve recognition of text meaning. When reading difficulties are present, saccades may be irregular in size, and regressive saccades — backward movements — occur more frequently.

By analyzing eye movement patterns — fixation duration, saccade count, and regression frequency — it is possible to identify not only surface-level reading problems (speed, accuracy) but also the underlying cognitive causes (attention, working memory, visual-perceptual processing).
